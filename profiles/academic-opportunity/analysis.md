# Evaluation goal

Evaluate academic opportunities (PhD/MSc funding, assistantships, scholarships, fellowships, summer schools, workshops, travel awards, research internships) for a researcher in mathematical biology, statistics, and operations research, interested in Canada (UBC, NSERC, Mitacs, CIHR, SSHRC) and major international funders (CSC, NIH, NSF, Wellcome, Simons).

The central question: can the reader actually apply, and is it worth applying?

# Personal Score rubric (0-10)

Score each dimension, then sum:

- **Importance (0-3).** Value of the opportunity itself: 3 = prestigious or fully funded program at a priority institution/funder; 2 = solid opportunity; 1 = minor; 0 = negligible.
- **Personal relevance (0-3).** Fit with the reader's fields (mathematical biology, computational biology, epidemiology, applied mathematics, statistics, operations research, AI for Science) and eligibility (career stage, international-student eligibility). 3 = strong fit and eligible; 0 = not applicable.
- **Actionability (0-1.5).** How actionable right now: 1.5 = open call with a clear deadline and application path; 1 = open but vague; 0.5 = announced for a future cycle; 0 = closed or informational only. Add urgency when the deadline is near.
- **Novelty (0-1).** Newly announced vs. recurring well-known program.
- **Credibility (0-1).** Source tier: A = official program/funder page (1.0); B = established academic listing (0.7); C = community repost (0.3); D = unverifiable (0).
- **Timeliness (0-0.5).** Deadline proximity: 0.5 = deadline within weeks; 0.25 = this cycle; 0 = distant or none.

# Scoring tiers

- **9-10: Apply now.** Excellent fit, eligible, open, deadline near.
- **7-8: Strong candidate.** Good fit, actionable this cycle.
- **5-6: Keep on radar.** Plausible fit or future cycle.
- **3-4: Weak fit.** Tangential field or unclear eligibility.
- **0-2: Not applicable.** Wrong field, wrong career stage, or marketing.

# Evaluation guidance

Always check: can the reader apply, what is the deadline, what are the eligibility constraints (citizenship, degree stage), the funding amount, the institution, and the research area. Never infer a deadline that is not stated. International-student eligibility matters: note when an opportunity is restricted (e.g. domestic only).

Use three to five specific topic tags.

# Output language

Write the reason and summary fields in Simplified Chinese. Tags may stay in English when they are technical terms.
