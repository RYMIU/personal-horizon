# Evaluation goal

Evaluate general scientific research for a researcher who wants only genuinely important results: high-quality journals, significant preprints, methodological innovation, large well-designed studies, and findings that could change a field's understanding.

# Personal Score rubric (0-10)

Score each dimension, then sum:

- **Importance (0-3).** Could this change how a field thinks or what it can do? 3 = potential field-changing result or major method; 2 = significant advance in an important area; 1 = solid incremental work; 0 = trivial.
- **Personal relevance (0-3).** Relevance to the reader's scientific environment: quantitative life sciences, statistics/ML, optimization, AI for Science, network science, computational methods. Work squarely in the reader's own niche topics is scored in `research-personal`, not here; score general relevance here. 3 = close to these areas; 0 = distant field.
- **Actionability (0-1.5).** Is it worth reading the paper, citing, or reusing the method or data?
- **Novelty (0-1).** New question, method, or definitive answer vs. incremental repetition.
- **Credibility (0-1).** Venue and evidence: A = original paper in a strong journal or well-received preprint (1.0); B = professional science media coverage of identified work (0.7); C = secondary/press-release only (0.3); D = unverifiable (0). Large samples, replication, and code/data availability raise confidence.
- **Timeliness (0-0.5).** Just published or newly posted.

# Scoring tiers

- **9-10: Landmark.** Results likely to be referenced for years; major methodological breakthroughs.
- **7-8: Important.** Significant advances worth reading in full.
- **5-6: Worth noting.** Solid work in relevant areas.
- **3-4: Low priority.** Incremental or narrow work.
- **0-2: Noise.** Press-release hype, minor findings, pop-science without substance.

# Evaluation guidance

A university press release is not evidence of importance; evaluate the underlying result, venue, design, and scale. Prefer the original paper as the primary link. Be skeptical of single small studies making sweeping claims.

Use three to five specific topic tags.

# Output language

Write the reason and summary fields in Simplified Chinese. Tags may stay in English when they are technical terms.
