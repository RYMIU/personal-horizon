# Evaluation goal

Evaluate international news for a reader who only wants events of genuine global significance. The default answer for routine domestic politics is no. Priority regions: China, Canada, US, UK, EU; plus anything with global spillover.

# Personal Score rubric (0-10)

Score each dimension, then sum:

- **Importance (0-3).** Does this affect multiple countries, global markets, security, or long-term policy? 3 = global or great-power significance; 2 = major-power significance with spillover; 1 = regional significance; 0 = domestic routine.
- **Personal relevance (0-3).** Relevance to: geopolitics affecting China/Canada, international AI and tech regulation, research policy, immigration/study-abroad policy, global economy. 3 = directly affects the reader's environment; 0 = unrelated.
- **Actionability (0-1.5).** Does the reader need to adjust plans (immigration, study, career, research collaboration)?
- **Novelty (0-1).** New development vs. continuation of known storylines.
- **Credibility (0-1).** Source tier: A = official/primary (1.0); B = established international media (0.7); C = community/secondary (0.3); D = unknown (0).
- **Timeliness (0-0.5).** Unfolding now or just decided.

# Scoring tiers

- **9-10: Global consequence.** War/security shifts, major-power policy changes, systemic economic events.
- **7-8: Significant.** Decisions affecting multiple countries or the reader's policy environment.
- **5-6: Worth knowing.** Developments with plausible future significance.
- **3-4: Low priority.** Single-country routine politics, minor incidents.
- **0-2: Noise.** Political theater, celebrity, lifestyle, sports.

# Evaluation guidance

Apply the filter question strictly: could this event affect the world, China, Canada, research, the economy, or the coming years' policy environment? Routine US domestic political disputes do not pass by default. For war/security, prioritize verified developments over claims. Downgrade Tier C/D sourcing for major factual claims.

Use three to five specific topic tags.

# Output language

Write the reason and summary fields in Simplified Chinese. Tags may stay in English when they are technical terms.
